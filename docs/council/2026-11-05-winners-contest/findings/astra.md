I submit **12 candidate claims: nine disjoint trade cohorts, one wallet cohort, and two behavioral entity candidates**. These are discovery findings, not verified October results. Several cohorts share one economic mechanism; nine profitable filters do not establish nine different causes.

Trade selectors were screened on August 1–September 7 fills whose markets had resolved before September 8, then tested on September 8–October 5 using only outcomes known before October 6. CIs below use 10,000 event-cluster bootstrap resamples. Wallet tests use September 7–October 4, allowing four complete weeks. The fixed, winner-enriched U_disc and truncated feeds prevent a fully independent population replay. Exploration covered 437 filter/metric specifications across five sweeps; intervals are generally pointwise. The actual contest holdout remains unseen.

The supported interpretations are **passive entry-price advantage, near-certainty carry, deadline/longshot mispricing, and information-based nowcasting**. Impatient traders can pay for immediacy; speculative buyers can overpay for unlikely outcomes; sellers can concede discounts to avoid settlement delay and residual risk. Culture profits principally involve box-office and audience-count markets, consistent with better nowcasts. The source of those traders' probability estimates is unobserved, so these are mechanisms consistent with the evidence, not established causal explanations.

Eight trade cohorts retain positive CIs after capping each historical fill's cost at $100. Daily crypto does not: its capped CI is [-$.29, $1.86]. One cent worse entry erases mentions and macro profits. Macro's largest event supplies 82% of profit; technology excludes 4,248 unresolved purchases versus only 972 resolved purchases. Geopolitics passes the requested 95% test but fails a twelve-test Bonferroni sensitivity. These limitations matter more than impressive win rates.

For W01, selecting ≥70% maker fills and ≥100 prior fills yields 232 wallets with mean **realized profit excluding incentives $58,660**, CI [$25,972, $97,604]. Exact pre-period P&L-decile/category controls exist for 191; excess is $43,586, with control-resampled CI [$7,375, $88,786]. Removing the five largest winners leaves positive mean and CI. Nevertheless, median profit is only $762 and median positive-week share 50%: this is not a uniformly steady maker strategy.

**The modest sweet spot**

A preselected $1k–$100k, ≥90%-positive-week, active cohort contains 47 wallets. Its test mean is $3,481, CI [$1,916, $5,381], with median 100% positive weeks and 85% positive days. It persists descriptively, but matched excess is **−$5,233**, CI [−$11,532, $167]. None of 21 sweet-spot variants meets the full control criterion; no sweet-spot claim is submitted.

Kalshi provides useful context, not validation: 23 of 1,186 profiles with discovery P&L meet the modest-profit/80%-positive-week/60%-positive-day pattern; 14 also have ≥30 observed active days. Public maker-heavy profiles look stronger descriptively. However, 5,181 profiles hide trades, fill fees are absent, and lifetime profiles are not valid discovery selectors. I do not infer a transferable Kalshi edge from these aggregates.

**Wallet networks**

E01 is a three-account small sports network. Before September 8, roughly 49–51% of each pair's smaller account fills matched within five seconds on market/outcome/side, with closely matching activity and P&L patterns. Its later combined economic P&L, including marks and income, is **$10,579**, mean $378/day, CI [$168, $698]; all five partial/complete weeks and 26/27 nonflat days are positive.

E02 is a separate three-account sports taker core: 33–34% matched co-fills, similar size patterns and balanced leading directions. Later economic P&L is **$41,975**, mean $1,499/day, CI [$807, $2,405]; 5/5 weeks and 21/28 days are positive. Both networks also have positive realized profit excluding incentives in every complete September test week.

These candidates were first noticed in full-discovery clusters, then their earlier links reconstructed: the tests are retrospective, not blinded. There is no shared-owner proof; common software or signals remain alternatives. A broader 19-wallet family using 55/110-share grids is an investigative lead. A six-wallet candidate was withheld because its later test boundary was chosen after overlapping profits had been inspected.

**What failed, and why copying fails**

Broad crypto-threshold buying, multiple late-entry crypto patterns, sports pregame/in-play rules, political deadline trades, equity takers, and sampled overreaction/momentum rules failed uncertainty or breadth checks. Cheap historical YES+NO pair costs did not establish profitable executable arbitrage. Weather depended heavily on one wallet; settlement-lag crypto weakened under size and concentration checks. Reward-history winners mostly earned trading profit, so I did not relabel them reward farming.

We can copy an address, not its fair-value model, hedge, inventory, queue position or original fill price. Public trades arrive after execution. A one-second operator needs its own signal, venue-specific fees/rules, price limits and execution testing; the $100 replay guarantees no fills. Five-minute and four-hour crypto are explicitly nonreplicable under the contest's venue mapping. Even seemingly portable carry can disappear with one tick. Winner selection, unresolved positions, account fragmentation and correlated bets further exaggerate what a leaderboard seems to promise.
