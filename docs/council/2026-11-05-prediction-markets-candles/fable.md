## 3. Answers

**Q1.** Rows 1–3 above. Note rows 1–2 are the same family: get paid for passivity on the venue with the wide spread and the rebate. Nobody pays a 1.3 s taker anything anymore — your own fill decay proved it in 48 hours.

**Q2.** Reproducible only in modified form. The original lives on international Polymarket's rebate engine, which you can't reach; Kalshi pays no rebate, and the Lancellotti simulation shows an alpha-less Kalshi maker loses outright. The closest analog: rest both sides on PM US hourly/daily brackets, lock when YES+NO < $1, hold to settlement (no merge needed — exactly one side pays $1). Confirm what the 25% rebate is actually 25% *of* before modeling it; the packet never says. The cross-venue version is worse, not better: at ~3% independent maker fill rates, most "pairs" are one-legged, and a one-legged maker is exactly the unhedged coin-flip that produced your ±$9–11 swings. Kill order: legging risk first, queue position second (you're behind incumbents), adverse selection third.

**Q3.** Yes, same phenomenon, and the trade is buy NO at 85–95c on long-dated upside thresholds. Honest sizing: +3–6c per 90c at risk, 5–10% loss frequency, weeks locked — maybe 15–25% annualized gross on the sleeve *if* the 2023 gap survived 90× volume growth, which is the thing to test, not assume. The VRP cuts the edge: option-implied digitals are themselves inflated by the variance premium, so the "true" gap vs real-world probability is smaller than 5.6pp. Hedging: Deribit and perps are out; delta-hedging a digital near strike is unbounded gamma — don't. The only hedge you actually have is a static spot overlay (US exchange) against clustered upside thresholds, plus diversification across coins/strikes/dates and a hard sleeve cap (~25% of bankroll). The -96c tail is the same lesson as Novig: never let one resolution be the sample.

**Q4.** No contradiction. The Kalshi study simulated a *one-sided, unrebated* maker; the census winners are *two-sided* (pair-lock caps the loss at combined-cost-minus-$1) and *rebated* (intl PM pays, Kalshi doesn't). Adverse selection of 0.6–0.9c/fill is real everywhere; on intl PM, spread-capture-when-both-sides-fill plus rebates covers it; on Kalshi nothing covers it. Reachable residue for you: only the pair-lock/rebate structure on PM US. Not reachable: Kalshi-leads-Binance (needs ms, and correlation≠tradable), cross-coin lead-lag (t=0.56, i.e., zero), streak reversal (53.1% ≈ 1.2c gross vs 2.5–3.5c round-trip — dead on arrival), volatility-conditioned lead pricing (real signal, but it's an input to quoting on a venue you can't access).

**Q5.** Ranking: (g) cross-instrument RV (PM vs CME-implied digitals — row 3, your genuinely differentiated seat) > (a) carry, but only the CME-futures-basis form (row 4); funding-rate arb proper needs perp access you don't have > (f) market making with rebates — yes on prediction markets (row 1), no on crypto CEXs at 1.3 s against colocated firms > (e) liquidation cascades — real (prior) but needs perp access and speed > (c) intraday momentum — literature is mixed and retail costs eat it > (d) cointegrated pairs — crypto cointegration is regime-unstable (prior) > (b) spot/perp cross-venue basis — no venue access. Candlestick patterns as such: no, they carry no edge beyond repackaged momentum/reversal, and the packet's own seasonality/TA evidence plus data-snooping literature says what survives is the underlying effect, never the pattern.

**Q6.** Best single combination: the longshot-NO book priced against CME option-implied digitals, with a static spot overlay on upside strikes. It uses the one asymmetry you have — a US-regulated seat where binaries trade against retail flow, benchmarked against an options market retail doesn't read. Binary-prices-as-spot-signal is backwards at your latency; buying "cheap convexity" fails because the replay data says favorites are *under*priced in the 60–90% zone and *over*priced above 97% — convexity buyers are the fish.

**Q7.** (1) +$1.98 over 10 hedged lots is statistically nothing; the four unhedged lots swung 10× that. You've measured execution variance, not edge. (2) Winner's-curse fills: at 5% fill rates, you fill precisely when someone faster declined — per-fill profit is conditional on a regime that already decayed once in two days. Markout analysis of existing fills is mandatory and free. (3) Maker fills n=4 at +$2–2.9: pure selection, not a rate. (4) Vendor census: survivorship-laden, sold by a bot vendor, median bot -$59, persistence 0.20 — the *expected* result of entering is negative. (5) Portnaya is Sept 2023; assume compression until re-measured. Before any capital: markouts, rebate mechanics, and a fresh PM-vs-CME gap measurement.

**Q8.** E1: markout audit of all existing fills (cost $0; kill: markout >50% of per-lot edge; P=40% the desk survives it). E2: PM US pair-lock live, $500, 2 weeks (criteria in table; P=35%). E3: 30-position PM-vs-CME gap measurement, then $1k longshot-NO pilot only if gap ≥3pp (P=25%). **Biggest program risk:** everything here pays single-digit dollars per day at best; the real cost is weeks of engineering attention spent defending a $10/day seat that one competent market maker deletes the day they arrive.

## 4. Top 3 risks

1. **Adverse-selection masquerading as edge** — the 1–5% fill residue may be systematically toxic; this invalidates rows 1, 2 and 5 simultaneously and is checkable for free.
2. **Stale evidence** — the only sizeable directional edge (longshot premium) is 2023 data in a market that grew 90×; deploying before re-measuring is betting on a ghost.
3. **Opportunity cost at scale** — even full success is ~$20–30/day on $10k; the program's EV is dominated by the time it consumes, not the money it risks.
